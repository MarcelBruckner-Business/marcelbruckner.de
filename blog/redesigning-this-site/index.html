Redesigning this site: four directions, then a newspaper

This site has a new look since this week: a name across the top like a newspaper, serif text for reading, one colour per area, and a light and a dark version. This post is about how it got there, because the first attempt was wrong in an instructive way.

What was wrong with the old one

Two things. The site looked like a template, and the blog looked like a changelog. Most posts were release notes of my projects, which arrive here automatically, and the few posts where I explain why I built something were buried between “0.31.1” and “0.32.0”.

The first attempt looked like my other site

I asked Claude for a redesign with the Open Design plugin, which produces mock-ups as plain HTML pages I can open on my phone. The first one was fine. It also looked very much like my 3D printing site, 3dbruckner.de, with different text.

That is what you get when you ask for “a redesign” without saying what the site is for. So I asked for several directions that differ on purpose, to have something to choose between.

  • Night signal (top left): dark and technical. I liked the mood.
  • Reading room (top right): the blog as the main thing. The right idea, but too quiet.
  • Allgäu (bottom left): mountains and warm colours. Too playful for me.
  • Index (bottom right): one big statement and a numbered list. The clearest of the four, and the best on a phone.

None of them was it, and each had one thing I wanted. So the next round mixed them, and one of the mixes had a centred name, a navigation line below it, one bold sentence and three coloured cards. That was the direction.

What the look is

  • Two typefaces with two jobs. A tight sans (Inter Tight) says what something is: statements, labels, numbers. A serif (Newsreader) tells the story: the name, the navigation, everything meant to be read.
  • One colour per area. Software is green, cloud is orange, 3D printing is pink, writing is blue. A colour only ever appears as a small square, a rule on top of a card, a number and an arrow.
  • Rules, not boxes. Sections are numbered and start with a line. Corners are sharp, and there are no shadows or gradients.
  • Light and dark. The site follows the device, and a switch at the top overrides it.

The switch remembers its setting in the browser’s local storage, not in a cookie, and the privacy page says so.

Essays first

The blog needed a rule more than a design. It is now this: a post tagged release is a release note, anything else is an essay. Essays get the page. Release notes sit in a side column, grouped by project, with the newest three each. The RSS feed carries essays only.

The posts still come from notefeed, one feed per topic, and the topic filter on the blog is simply the feed a post came from.

What the phone showed me

The mock-ups looked right. Then the real site was live and I opened it on my iPhone:

  • The arrow next to external links, a plain ↗ character, was drawn as a blue emoji. Text characters that have an emoji twin are not safe as icons. The site now uses real icons from Lucide.
  • The navigation wrapped so that one link sat alone on a second line. It is now a grid of three by two on small screens.
  • The order of the home page felt off: what I do, then numbers, then who I am, with parts repeating. “Who I am” moved up next to the opening statement, and the separate section went away.

None of this showed up in the mock-ups. I had looked at them, not used them.

Not one brand for everything

Halfway through I wondered whether this should become the look for everything I make: this site, the 3D printing site, notefeed, Gamepanion. It sounded tidy. I decided against it. A newspaper front page suits a site that is mostly writing. A shop needs its photos to carry the page, and a product needs to look like itself. So this look stays here, the 3D printing site gets its own design next, and the products keep theirs.

While I was at it

The content was as dated as the design. Several products were missing from the projects page, and the cloud page still described one server where there are two. The cloud page now follows the repository in which I keep that setup as code. There is also a new section for contributions to other people’s projects, and the text and my own pictures in these posts are now under CC BY 4.0.

The whole redesign was one long conversation, most of it spoken, and four pull requests.
